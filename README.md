Project Overview
This report provides a comprehensive analysis of the Home Loan dataset, examining the demographic, financial, and behavioral factors that influence loan approvals and rejections. The dataset includes key information such as applicant income, credit history, loan amount, gender, marital status, and property type. Through this analysis, we aim to uncover patterns that help financial institutions optimize lending decisions, mitigate risk, and improve customer satisfaction.
________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________
Dataset Description
The Home Loan dataset includes the following key attributes:
- Gender: Applicant’s gender
- Married: Marital status
- Dependents: Number of dependents
- Education: Education level
- Self_Employed: Employment type
- ApplicantIncome: Monthly income of applicant
- LoanAmount: Loan amount requested
- Loan_Amount_Term: Duration of loan in months
- Credit_History: Credit repayment record (1 = Premium, 0 = Normal)
- Loan_Status: Whether the loan was approved or not

Business Objectives
The primary goals of this project are to:
1. Identify key financial and demographic factors affecting loan approval rates.
2. Analyze income patterns and their relationship with loan amounts.
3. Examine the impact of credit history and property area on loan approvals.
4. Suggest actionable insights for improving approval efficiency and risk management.
________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________
Key Business Questions
This analysis explores several essential questions:
- Which income groups are more likely to get loan approval?
- How does credit history affect loan decisions?
- What role does education or employment type play in approval rates?
- Which property area has the highest loan acceptance?
- Are married applicants more likely to have their loans approved?

Tools & Techniques
The following tools and techniques were used for data processing and analysis:
- Power BI for interactive dashboarding and visualization.
- Power Query for data cleaning and transformation.
- DAX measures to compute loan approval ratios, average incomes, and trends.
- Data modeling using relationship mapping between categorical and numerical variables.

Key Insights
1. Applicants with a good credit history (Credit_History = 1) have an approval rate nearly 4 times higher than those with poor history.
2. Married applicants and those with higher co-applicant income tend to secure larger loans and have better approval odds.
3. Self-employed individuals show slightly lower approval rates due to income variability, despite higher average incomes.
4. Urban and semiurban property areas dominate approvals, reflecting financial institutions’ lower risk perception in these zones.
5. Education influences financial literacy: Graduates tend to manage credit better and get higher loan amounts.
6. Loan amounts are moderately correlated with total applicant income, though credit history remains the strongest predictor.
